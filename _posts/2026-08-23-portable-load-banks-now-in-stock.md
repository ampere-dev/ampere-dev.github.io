### Technical Function of a Portable Load Bank

A load bank is a self-contained device that develops a precise electrical load, applies it to a power source, and dissipates the resulting energy—typically as heat—while monitoring voltage, current, power, and related parameters. Unlike facility or process loads, which are dispersed, variable, and often critical, a load bank presents a known, controllable, and non-productive burden. This allows operators to:

- Verify continuous power capability under steady-state conditions
- Confirm step-load response and transient recovery
- Measure voltage regulation, frequency stability, and harmonic content
- Exercise batteries through controlled discharge profiles
- Validate protective-device settings and cooling-system performance

Portable designs compress these functions into enclosures that fit through standard doorways, elevators, and vehicle cargo spaces. Many units include integrated wheels and carrying cases so that a single technician can move the equipment from a service vehicle to the test location without mechanical assistance.

The newly stocked fleet is organized by application domain:

**DC Portable Load Banks**  
Voltage range typically 12–270 VDC with current capacities extending to 2000 A on selected models. These units support battery discharge testing, UPS string validation, telecom backup systems, motive-power batteries, and 28 V aircraft ground-power units. Fine current resolution (often 20 A steps or continuous adjustment) enables accurate capacity verification and state-of-health assessment without risk of over-discharge.

**60 Hz Portable Load Banks**  
Single-phase and three-phase configurations at common distribution voltages (120/240 V and 208/480 V). Continuous ratings reach 100 kW with load-step resolution as fine as 1 kW on many models. Digital metering and optional data logging capture real and reactive power, power factor, voltage, current, and frequency for commissioning records and annual maintenance reports. These units are sized for the majority of commercial standby generators, temporary power plants, and smaller industrial UPS systems.

**400 Hz Portable Load Banks**  
Specialized for aircraft ground power units (GPUs), gate boxes, and military applications. Continuous ratings extend to 120 kW with stepped or continuous loading. The higher frequency imposes stricter requirements on load-element design, cooling, and instrumentation accuracy; the portable units in inventory meet these constraints while remaining transportable by a single operator.

All units incorporate overload protection, thermal monitoring, and clear local displays. Select models offer Cam-Lok connectors, remote-control interfaces, and data-logging capability for integration into formal test protocols.

### Engineering Advantages of the Portable Form Factor

- **Access and footprint** — Dimensions and weight are constrained so that equipment can be positioned adjacent to the unit under test, minimizing cable lengths and associated voltage drop.
- **Setup time** — Plug-and-play connections and intuitive controls allow testing to begin within minutes of arrival, reducing labor hours and schedule impact.
- **Resolution and controllability** — Fine load steps or continuous adjustment enable precise matching to generator capability curves, battery C-rates, or inverter limits.
- **Safety isolation** — Built-in protective circuits and forced-air cooling maintain safe surface temperatures and prevent thermal runaway of load elements.
- **Documentation** — Metering accuracy and optional logging support generation of test reports that satisfy manufacturer warranty requirements, regulatory inspections, and internal quality systems.

### Application Domains

Portable load banks are particularly effective in the following environments:

- Commissioning and annual load-bank testing of small-to-medium diesel and natural-gas generators
- Runtime and battery-health verification of UPS systems serving critical but relatively low-power loads
- Discharge capacity testing of telecom, data-center edge, and industrial battery strings
- Shore-power and onboard-generator validation for yachts, commercial vessels, and dockside facilities
- 400 Hz GPU and ground-power testing at airports and military installations
- Temporary power and event-generator acceptance testing
- Field service and preventive-maintenance programs where equipment must be moved between multiple sites in a single day

In each case the ability to apply a controlled load on-site eliminates the risks associated with using live facility loads and provides quantitative performance data that cannot be obtained from no-load or light-load runs.
